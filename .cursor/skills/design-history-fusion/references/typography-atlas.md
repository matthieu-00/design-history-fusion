# Typography Atlas

Organized by structural category (Vox-ATypI-style) rather than strict decade, since revivals break the decade model. Use this to pick a *category* fast, then verify a specific named typeface via web_search/Fonts In Use before committing to it in a deliverable — foundries, exact release years, and licensing all need checking.

| Category | Era most associated | Feel it carries into a UI | Examples to verify before use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Humanist Sans | 1920s roots, 1990s–2010s dominant on screens | Warm, approachable, high legibility at small sizes | Gill Sans-style, later humanist-sans web faces |
| Geometric Sans | 1920s–30s (Bauhaus/Art Deco), 2010s revival | Confident, modern, slightly retro-futurist when pushed | Futura-style, Kabel-style, 2010s geometric-sans revivals |
| Grotesque / Neo-Grotesque | 1950s–60s (Swiss Style), 2000s revival | Neutral, objective, "invisible" carrier of content | Helvetica-style, Univers-style neo-grotesques |
| Slab (Egyptian/Ionic) | 19th c. origin, 1970s New York School revival | Bold, confident, editorial/poster energy | Clarendon-style, Rockwell-style |
| Didone (high-contrast serif) | Late 18th c. origin, Art Deco-adjacent revival | Fashion-editorial, luxury, high contrast | Bodoni-style, Didot-style |
| Script/Hand-lettered | Art Nouveau (1900s–10s), psychedelia (1960s), grunge (1990s) | Organic, expressive, anti-corporate | Varies heavily by decade — always verify a named script face |
| Display/Deconstructed | Postmodern (1980s), grunge (1990s) | Loud, layered, intentionally imperfect | Emigre-foundry-era faces, Weingart-influenced display type |

## Quick decade → category shortcut
- 1900s–10s: split — Script/hand-lettered ornamental (French Art Nouveau) vs. Geometric restraint (Vienna Secession) — these are opposite registers, not one look
- 1920s: split — Geometric Sans/pedagogical (Bauhaus), political-diagonal (Constructivism), grid-primary (De Stijl), decorative-geometric (Art Deco) — four distinct currents, not one
- 1930s: Geometric Sans, early Humanist Sans
- 1940s–50s: split — Grotesque/Neo-Grotesque, Swiss discipline (Müller-Brockmann) vs. looser American Mid-Century commercial decoration — related but not the same tradition
- 1960s: split — Script/psychedelic hand-lettering vs. continued Neo-Grotesque
- 1970s: three distinct currents, not one — Slab/extended-display phototype precision (corporate/NY School), Script/hand-lettered airbrush glamour (disco), Display/collage ransom-note DIY (punk). Don't default to earth-tone bubble lettering for all three.
- 1980s: Display/Deconstructed, Geometric Sans (postmodern reinterpretation)
- 1990s: Display/Deconstructed (grunge)
- 2000s: Grotesque/Neo-Grotesque revival
- 2010s: Geometric Sans revival, Humanist Sans dominant for UI body text
- 2020s: split — bold, high-contrast Grotesque/Geometric Sans (confident minimalism) vs. Display/Deconstructed Y2K-chrome and scrapbook-collage revival; return-to-serif current running in parallel

## Living-edge note
The 2020s row above will lag actual practice — verify anything from the last ~3 years via web_search (Fonts In Use, It's Nice That, or similar current-practice sources) rather than treating this file as settled fact for the current decade.

## Non-Latin scripts — not covered by the table above
This table (and Vox-ATypI generally) only classifies Latin-script typefaces. Japanese typography (see `timeline.md`'s Japanese Graphic Design entry) operates under different structural rules — kanji, hiragana, and katakana combine within a single sentence, and ATypI itself de-adopted Vox-ATypI in 2021 partly because it flattened every non-Latin script into a single undifferentiated bucket. Don't force a Japanese-inspired brief into a Latin category above; treat it as its own tradition with its own two poles (Kamekura's geometric restraint vs. Yokoo's maximalist collage).

## Fusion pairing patterns that tend to work
- Period **display** face (any era above) + contemporary **variable humanist sans** for body/UI text — keeps the historical signal loud where it's decorative and keeps small-size legibility/accessibility fully modern.
- Period **color + texture** with a fully modern **grotesque/geometric sans** typeface — signal comes from palette and ornament, not the letterforms, which is often the more usable choice for dense UI.
- Avoid pairing two loud display eras together (e.g., 1970s slab + 1980s deconstructed) unless the brief explicitly wants maximalist collage — it reads as noise rather than intentional fusion.

## Watch list — combos that started deliberate and are becoming this skill's own default
Fraunces (display) + Inter (body) is a genuinely good pairing and was the right call for Manna's 1970s option — but it's also the single most common "vintage-meets-modern" font pairing in AI design output right now, this skill included. If a session reaches for it again without a specific reason tied to that era/brief, treat that as the same flattening problem covered in `references/anti-slop.md`, not a safe default. Rotate: Fraunces isn't the only warm/period-adjacent display face — consider what the specific era's typography atlas entry actually calls for (slab, geometric sans, Didone, script) before reaching for the familiar choice.
